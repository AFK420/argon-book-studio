# Cormac Vane: The Alchemical Inquest
*Being the Case of the Salt-Cellar Murders*
*By A. E. Sterling & The Argon Studio*

## Chapter 1: The Precipitate at Low Water

The Thames did not surrender its dead so much as pickle them. At quarter past three on a sulphur-yellow Tuesday, Cormac Vane stepped beneath the lime-washed arches of Wapping Basin with a horn-handled magnifying glass tucked into his waistcoat and three drops of oil of wintergreen dabbed beneath each nostril.

The river police had laid the body across four wet deal planks. The derrick chains above still groaned in the sleet, swinging like pendulum bobs against the black hull of an anchored collier.

"He was not drowned, Inspector," Vane said, kneeling without minding the river sludge that seeped into his trouser cuffs. "Drowning leaves frothy mucus in the trachea and dilates the right ventricle. Observe the fingers. The nails are stained with antimony tartrate, and the skin around the cuticles has turned the pale saffron of dry sublimate."

Inspector Hallowell rubbed a chilblained ear with his woolen glove. "The dockers found him wedged between the wharf piles. They swear he was one of the glass-blowers from the Ratcliffe Highway furnaces."

"Dockers swear to whatever shortens their interviews in the sleet," Vane replied smoothly. He dipped a bone spatula into the small crystal vial he carried in his pocket, then pressed the moistened tip against the deceased's tongue.

Vane straightened his back and wiped the spatula with a clean piece of linen. A shudder ran along his spine—not from the north-easter biting through his overcoat, but from the sudden, sharp prickling behind his ears that always accompanied a sensory draw.

"He was killed indoors," Vane said softly. "Carried down the stairs in a wool blanket while the frost was still forming on the flagstones. Then dropped through the coal chute into the slipway."

***

Hallowell signaled to the watermen to cover the planks with tarpaulin. "If you are right, Mr. Vane, the furnace at No. 7 Orchard Lane has been burning throughout the night."

"It has never ceased burning," Vane answered, gazing past the coal barges toward the smoky spires of Shadwell. "And if we do not lay our hands upon the apothecary's ledger before the tide turns again, every drop of Thames water between here and Greenwich will bear the bitter taste of silver."

The two men stepped into the four-wheeler cab waiting at the foot of Wapping Stairs, the iron-shod wheels rattling against the cobblestones as the yellow fog rolled inland.

## Chapter 2: The Alembic in the Shadows

The cellar below Orchard Lane smelled of vitriol and cabbage soup. Vane held the bull's-eye lantern at shoulder height, casting an amber disc across rows of glass retorts, green carboys wrapped in wicker, and jars of pickled organs whose labels had rotted into brown lace.

On the workbench lay a copper balance scale, its needle trembling at a tenth of a grain.

"Do not touch the dust upon the rim," Vane warned as Hallowell reached out. "The crystals are efflorescent. A single breath will scatter enough arsenic trioxide to blind us both."

Beside the scales sat a bound ledger with corners capped in oxidized brass. Vane drew on a pair of kidskin gloves and carefully opened the vellum pages.

"Look here," he murmured, his lantern beam pinpointing three lines of Latin script penned in iron gall ink. "The names are not customers. They are donors of raw marrow."

Hallowell's face went white in the amber light. "Good God, Vane. You mean the apothecary was refining the salt from living bone?"

Vane closed the book with a muffled thud. "Not living bone, Inspector. Just what remained after the inquest was signed."
