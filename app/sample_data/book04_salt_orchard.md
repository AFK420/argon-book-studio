# The Salt Orchard of Saint Jude
*Field Studies in Marsh Necrosis and Deep Root Glow*
*By Cordelia Vane Pelham & The Argon Studio*

## Chapter 1: The Luminous Taproot

The tide came into the spartina marsh not as clean seawater, but as an oily, phosphorescent skim that coated the stems of the black needlerush in a cold amber grease. Cordelia waded through the pluff mud with her hip-waders hitched to her brass belt-rings and her galvanized zinc specimen cans slung across her collarbone.

Behind her, the silhouette of the abandoned rice mill stood against the copper sunset like the skeleton of a grounded galleon.

"You should not touch the aerial roots after sundown, Miss Pelham," old Gideon called from the stern of the flat-bottomed skiff, his cedar pole dripping green marsh-rot back into the creek. "The salt-apple doesn't bloom for bees. It blooms for the swamp-flies that feed on rotting stingray."

Cordelia used her pruning knife to slit a three-inch flap in the bark of an ancient bald cypress whose buttress had drowned in the encroaching seawater. A thick, gelatinous sap welled into the wound—glowing with a quiet, mustard-colored bioluminescence that cast the veins of her palm in sharp relief.

"The trees are converting the ocean salt into a hydrocarbon polymer," she whispered, collecting the sap in a wide-mouthed amber jar. "It isn't a disease, Gideon. It is an adaptation. The marsh is calcifying itself against the sea."

Gideon pushed the skiff against the cypress knees, the boat grinding into the shell hash. "Call it what you like, Miss Cordelia. But my father helped dig the drainage canals in seventy-two, and he swore the roots were whispering in the dark before the fever took the overseer."

***

A low heron croaked in the cordgrass. Above the marsh, the first evening stars appeared through the humid coastal haze, mirrored below by the thousands of glowing amber root-tips extending through the dark black mud.

Cordelia tied the specimen jar into her canvas knapsack and signaled to the boatman. "Row us back to the landing, Gideon. Tomorrow morning we test the fruit."

## Chapter 2: The Marsh Laboratory

The kitchen of the old overseer's house had been converted into an impromptu laboratory. Glass petri dishes covered the long pine harvest table, and a kerosene stove kept two copper sterilizers at a steady simmer.

Cordelia sliced a thin cross-section of the salt-apple using a straight razor. Under the brass tube of her Bausch & Lomb microscope, the cellular structure was startling: the xylem vessels were lined with microscopic hexagonal crystals of pure aragonite.

"The tree is building stone skeletons inside its wood," she muttered to the empty room.
