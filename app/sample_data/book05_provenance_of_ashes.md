# Provenance of Ashes
*Neutron Activation and the Nineveh Smuggler's Tablets*
*By T. A. Qadir & The Argon Studio*

## Chapter 1: The Epigrapher's Ash

The clay tablet arrived at the laboratory inside a tin biscuit box packed with shredded Syrian newspapers from 1958. Tariq Qadir lifted the piece with cotton-tipped tweezers, holding his breath so that not even the warm moisture of his lungs might soften the unbaked sun-dried silt.

It was no larger than a child's hand, pillow-shaped, colored the pale yellow-buff of Tigris riverbed loam. Across its convex surface ran thirty-two lines of minute cuneiform wedges, crisp as though pressed yesterday into fresh butter.

"The auction catalog listed it as an Old Babylonian land grant from Sippar," Dr. Qadir said, sliding the lead shield of the X-ray fluorescence spectrometer into place. "The dealer swore on his father's grave that it came from a private collection formed before the mandate."

Maryam leaned over the console, the amber CRT reflecting in her dark irises. "And the spectrometer?"

"The clay contains point two percent barium sulfate and three parts per million europium," Qadir said calmly. "Europium isotopes are not found in the alluvium of southern Mesopotamia. They are found in the tailings of the phosphate mines near Mosul."

"It isn't a land grant," Maryam breathed, reading over his shoulder. "This is a fragment of the Erra and Ishum cycle. The lost third tablet of the Ashurbanipal recension."

"Or a forgery so brilliant that only neutron activation can prove whether the stylus was pressed by a Nineveh scribe in 650 BC or by an art student in Baghdad three months ago," Qadir answered.

He opened a velvet-lined case on the table. Inside rested a splinter of dark, fragrant wood.

"The core contains authentic 7th-century wood ash," Qadir said, his voice dropping. "Whoever baked this tablet didn't forge the clay. They melted an ancient royal archive to hide the isotope signature inside."

***

The gamma-ray detector clicked softly in the lead vault behind them—a rhythmic, indifferent countdown measuring the decay of half-lives that had begun when Nineveh still burned against the desert sky.

Qadir took off his cotton gloves and laid them beside the brass caliper. "Call the Antiquities Directorate, Maryam. Tell them we have found the provenance. But the ashes do not belong to the past."

## Chapter 2: The Isotope Matrix

The second analysis took six hours. Outside the university basement, the autumn wind tossed plane-tree leaves against the frosted high windows.

On the laboratory monitor, the spectral peaks of thorium and lanthanum clustered like constellations in a midnight sky.

"The signature matches the lower strata of Tell Brak," Maryam said, pointing to the isotope ratio curve. "Where the palace of Naram-Sin was burned during the Gutian invasion."
